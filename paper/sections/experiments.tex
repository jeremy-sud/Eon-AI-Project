\section{Experiments and Performance Benchmarks}

We evaluate Eón across four evaluation axes: (1) chaotic sequence forecasting accuracy under fixed-point quantization, (2) computational complexity and training latency of the Cholesky solver, (3) real-world edge application prototypes (bio-medical, acoustic, and language), and (4) wireless energy consumption under the 1-Bit protocol.

\subsection{Chaotic Time-Series Benchmark (Mackey-Glass)}
We evaluate temporal prediction on the standard Mackey-Glass non-linear delay differential benchmark ($\tau=17$, producing a chaotic strange attractor):
\begin{equation}
    \frac{dx}{dt} = \frac{0.2 \, x(t - \tau)}{1 + x(t - \tau)^{10}} - 0.1 \, x(t)
\end{equation}
The dataset comprises $2000$ points ($1500$ training, $500$ testing, $100$ initial washout steps). An Eón reservoir with $N=50$ neurons ($\alpha=0.3, \rho=0.9, p=0.2$) is evaluated across arithmetic representations.

\subsection{On-Device Prototype Deployments}

\subsubsection{Eón Bio: Privacy-Preserving Arrhythmia Monitoring}
We deployed the Eón C core on a wearable microcontroller testbed analyzing continuous electrocardiogram RR-intervals from the MIT-BIH Arrhythmia Database. Using an ultralight reservoir of $N=20$ neurons ($1.3\,\text{KB}$ RAM), the system self-calibrates to the patient's individual Respiratory Sinus Arrhythmia (RSA) within $50$ cardiac cycles. 
\textbf{Results:} Eón achieved $100\%$ detection sensitivity for Premature Ventricular Contractions (PVCs) by tracking single-step prediction error residuals ($\Delta > 20\%$), operating at an average active power consumption of $50\,\mu\text{W}$ without transmitting raw medical waveforms off-device.

\subsubsection{Eón Voice: Microcontroller Keyword Spotting (KWS)}
Acoustic data was converted into 4 mel-frequency energy bands sampled at $50\,\text{Hz}$. An Eón reservoir ($N=100$) was trained using the online NLMS delta rule to detect the temporal utterance of target wake-words.
\textbf{Results:} The system demonstrated detection confidence $>0.85$ with a false alarm rate below $0.05$ per hour in the presence of $15\,\text{dB}$ background ambient noise, executing within $65\,\mu\text{s}$ per acoustic frame.

\subsubsection{TinyLM: Word-Level Embeddings via Closed-Form Digital Root}
For symbolic text prediction on microcontrollers without Flash space for multi-megabyte embedding dictionaries, Eón combines an $N=128$ reservoir with an algorithmic hash layer based on closed-form digital roots:
\begin{equation}
    dr(w) = 1 + \left( \left( \sum_{b \in w} b - 1 \right) \bmod 9 \right)
\end{equation}
\textbf{Results:} Tokens are projected into phase-modulated sinusoidal basis vectors with zero lookup-table memory. Combined with Trie-based prefix compression, TinyLM reduces memory by $>65\%$ compared to standard embedded word vectors while preserving next-token ranking accuracy.
